Hi, I'm Vighnesh. This is Recoup, my submission for Track 3, AI Revenue Recovery.

UPI autopay fails constantly. Most of the time the reason is boring. The account was empty on debit day. That's it. But it costs subscription businesses five to fifteen percent of their recurring revenue.

Recovery today doesn't work. Dunning systems retry on a fixed schedule and hit the same empty account. And nobody's going to spend fifty rupees of call-center time chasing a four-ninety-nine subscription. So the money gets written off.

The frustrating part? The customer usually wants to pay. Ask them and they'll tell you exactly when. "Salary aane ke baad pakka kar dunga."

No system today can do anything with that sentence. Recoup can.

It's an agent that calls the customer, talks in normal Hinglish, gets that promise, converts the vague phrase into an actual date and time, and retries the payment at that moment. When the money is actually there.

Let me run it on a batch of eight failed mandates. About twelve thousand rupees at risk.

(first conversation) This is Asha, our agent, talking to Ramesh. His gym payment bounced. He says "salary aane ke baad pakka kar dunga." Now watch this line. (pause on the parse) We know his salary lands on the first, so the parser schedules the retry for September second, eleven AM. That's a deterministic rule, not the model guessing. It even carries a confidence score.

(next) Priya says "parso shaam ko." Day after tomorrow, evening. Scheduled for the twenty-second at six PM.

(opt-out) Imran is on the do-not-call list. The agent never dials. (revoked) Deepak revoked his mandate. We never retry it. (dispute) Meena says the charge is wrong. The agent doesn't argue. It hands off to a human and freezes every retry.

Rule R3 blocks the contact before it happens, and this rule lives in plain code outside the model. You cannot prompt your way around it.

(scheduler) Then the scheduler fires each retry at the promised time. One customer breaks her promise. Her retry fails, her cap is exhausted, and the system stops. Correctly. Everyone else pays when they said they would.

The number: seven thousand and ninety-six rupees recovered out of twelve thousand and ninety-two at risk. Fifty-nine percent. Total cost of getting it back, thirty-two rupees fifty.

And every action landed in an append-only audit log with the rule that allowed or blocked it. So when someone asks why the agent called this customer at this hour, there's an answer on file.

How it works: a free-tier LLM with tool calling runs the conversation, a rule-based parser handles the Hinglish time phrases, and the RBI-style guardrails sit outside the model entirely. Contact hours, weekly caps, retry limits, pre-debit notice.

In production the scheduler calls Razorpay's mandate re-presentation API, and the same loop drives a real voice stack.

Recoup takes "salary ke baad kar dunga" and turns it into a scheduled, compliant, auditable retry. Written-off failures become revenue again.
